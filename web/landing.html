Atomic swap · P2P OTC · Native Zcash ↔ Ethereum

Swap shielded ZEC for ETH.
No bridge. No wrapped ZEC.

Zatomic is an adaptor-signature atomic swap between native shielded ZEC and ETH. Before funding, both owners prepare and verify their exits: Bob a pre-signed ZEC claim that only a secret t can complete, Alice a signed refund that unlocks later. Claiming the ETH reveals t and completes Bob’s claim; if Alice never claims, each refunds after their own deadline.

Built for negotiated OTC swaps: two parties who have agreed a price and want native shielded settlement, with no bridge, custodian, solver or liquidity pool in between.

How it works

Four steps, one secret

What is new: Zcash’s own shielded spend authorization becomes one side of an atomic swap. Ethereum checks the same Pallas secret that completes the pre-signed Zcash payment, without verifying a Zcash block, seeing a Zcash recipient or minting a wrapped asset. FROST DKG and nonces come from the Zcash Foundation libraries; the adaptor extension and its Solidity check are this project’s own and unaudited.

  1. 01

    Lock a point

    Alice picks a fresh secret scalar t and shares only T = t·G on the Pallas curve.

  2. 02

    Prepare both exits first

    A fresh 2-of-2 FROST key controls the ZEC. Bob gets an adaptor pre-signature ŝ for claim C. Alice gets refund R, fully signed and time-locked.

  3. 03

    Claim ETH by revealing

    After the ZEC is funded and Bob locks ETH, Alice calls claim(t). The escrow checks t·G = T and credits her fixed address.

  4. 04

    Complete natively

    Bob reads t and computes s = ŝ + t, a valid RedPallas spend signature. His shielded claim needs nothing more from Alice.

Transaction by transaction

  1. Owners, off chainZcashEthereum
  2. Setup: nothing on chain yetAlice signs a quote and Bob accepts. A FROST DKG gives them a joint 2-of-2 key. Bob receives ŝ, an adaptor pre-signature on his claim C that becomes a valid RedPallas signature only with t. Alice holds her refund R, fully signed but valid only after the refund height. Both sign the full terms.
  3. F · Alice funds the joint noteShielded. Only Alice and Bob together can spend it.
  4. Lock · Bob locks the ETHFor Alice, claimable only with t where t·G = T, until the ETH deadline.
  5. Claim · Alice takes the ETHBefore the ETH deadline. The transaction publishes t.
  6. C · Bob takes the ZECs = ŝ + t completes his signature. Shielded, before the refund height.
  7. Withdraw · Alice withdrawsThe claim credits her in the escrow; this pays it out.
  8. If Alice never claims
  9. Refund · Bob refunds, then withdrawsAfter the ETH deadline. t was never published.
  10. A, then R · Alice’s ZEC comes backAfter the refund height. A turns 20,000 zatoshi of her change into a transparent coin; R spends it, because Zcash enforces a time lock only on a transparent input. Her ZEC returns shielded.

Why two deadlines, in this order. The ETH deadline comes first and the Zcash refund height later. Once Alice publishes t, Bob still has time to claim the ZEC before her refund can unlock, as long as his app checks in. That window is the monitoring assumption. Protocol details ↗

Bridges · NEAR Intents · THORChain · hashlocks

Why not an existing route?

Each route works. NEAR Intents and THORChain find liquidity and a price for you. Zatomic settles a price two parties already agreed, directly, with the ZEC leg shielded. It offers no price discovery or matching, covers ZEC ⇄ ETH only, and needs each owner to watch until the deadlines.

Not claimed

  • One success-path swap ran on the public testnets; refunds and crash recovery were shown on isolated local chains. No mainnet swap.
  • Both owners run on one machine. Independent hosts are not demonstrated.
  • The custom adaptor signature has no external audit or formal proof.
  • ETH amounts and addresses are public, as is the revealed witness. A refund (only a refund) adds transparent outputs on Zcash: a 20,000-zatoshi coin and its 15,000-zatoshi change.
  • Safety needs monitoring within the timing window; long outages or deep reorgs can break it.
