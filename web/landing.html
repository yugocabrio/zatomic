Atomic swap · P2P OTC · Native Zcash ⇄ Ethereum

Swap shielded ZEC for ETH.
No bridge. No wrapped ZEC.

An atomic swap needs a payment that unlocks on a condition and a refund that unlocks later, but shielded Zcash notes have no scripts for either. Zatomic prepares both exits before any money moves: Bob’s claim, signed except for one secret, and Alice’s refund, time-locked by Zcash itself. When Alice reveals the secret to take the ETH, it completes Bob’s Zcash signature.

The problem

Why a shielded atomic swap is hard

Two people want to trade ZEC for ETH without a custodian and without giving up Zcash privacy.

  1. 01

    Neither side wants to go first

    Whoever pays first can be cheated, so the two payments must be linked: both happen, or neither does.

  2. 02

    Shielded notes have no scripts

    Bitcoin-style swaps lock coins behind hash and time conditions. A shielded Zcash note moves only with a valid signature from its key.

  3. 03

    The usual workarounds leak

    Bridges prove Zcash history to Ethereum; other routes use solvers, public viewing keys or transparent ZEC. How they compare

The idea

One secret, two exits prepared before funding

What is new: Zatomic makes Zcash’s own shielded spend authorization one side of an atomic swap. Neither chain verifies the other’s transactions.

  • A key neither can use alone

    A FROST DKG gives Alice and Bob a fresh 2‑of‑2 spend key. Alice funds a shielded note that only both together could spend.

  • A claim one secret short

    Bob holds claim C, signed with a RedPallas adaptor signature that becomes valid only when a secret t is added.

  • A refund Zcash unlocks

    Alice holds refund R, fully signed, which Zcash consensus keeps invalid until her deadline. She needs nothing from Bob to use it.

Why t completes Bob’s signature

A RedPallas signature (R, s) for key X and message m, with challenge c = H(R, X, m), verifies when

s·G = R + c·X

Bob’s pre-signature is one secret short, with T = t·G:

ŝ·G = R̂ − T + c·X′

Adding t gives s = ŝ + t, so s·G = R̂ + c·X′: an ordinary valid signature for the randomized spend key X′. Ethereum checks t·G = T on Pallas itself, so the same t needs no cross-curve proof.

How both exits exist before the note does

In Zcash’s v6 transaction format (ZIP 229), the anchor and the zero-knowledge proof are authorizing data, outside what a spend signature signs. So C and R are signed before Alice funds the note, then proved against the real anchor once F is mined, with no new signature from either side.

Both exits spend the same note, so they share one nullifier: once one is mined, the other can never be.

How it works

Five steps, one secret

The settled path, in the order the two apps run it. Each app holds only its owner’s keys.

  1. 01

    Agree on the trade and prepare both exits Off chain

    Alice signs a quote with exact ZEC and ETH amounts, and Bob accepts. A FROST DKG gives them a fresh 2-of-2 spend key, so neither can spend the shared ZEC alone. Alice picks a secret scalar t and shares only T = t·G on Pallas. Before any funds move, Bob verifies his adaptor pre-signature ŝ on claim C, Alice holds refund R fully signed and time-locked, and both sign the full terms.

  2. 02

    Alice funds the shared shielded note Zcash · F

    Transaction F moves Alice’s ZEC into the note the joint FROST key controls. It has no transparent input or output, and the note’s only two exits, Bob’s claim and Alice’s time-locked refund, were authorized before funding.

  3. 03

    Bob locks ETH in the escrow Ethereum · lock

    After checking F, Bob locks the agreed ETH in PointEscrow for Alice’s fixed address and the point T, until the ETH deadline. The escrow receives no Zcash transaction ID, address or note.

  4. 04

    Alice claims the ETH and reveals t Ethereum · claim

    Alice calls claim(t). The escrow checks t·G = T and credits the ETH to Alice, who withdraws it in a separate transaction. The claim makes t public, and t is exactly what Bob’s signature is missing.

  5. 05

    Bob completes his shielded claim Zcash · C

    Bob reads t from Ethereum and computes s = ŝ + t, a valid RedPallas spend signature. He broadcasts claim C and receives the ZEC, shielded, with nothing more needed from Alice.

Result: two native settlements, linked by one scalar. On Zcash the whole swap is F and C, both fully shielded.

If Alice never reveals t

Bob takes his ETH back after the ETH deadline. After the later Zcash refund height, Alice broadcasts her pre-signed transaction A, which turns a little of her own change into a small transparent coin. She then broadcasts her time-locked refund R, which spends that coin and returns her ZEC shielded. Zcash enforces a lock time only on a transparent input; a settled swap needs neither A nor R.

Why the ETH deadline comes first: once Alice reveals t, Bob still has time to claim the ZEC before her refund unlocks. Each app watches its deadline; recovery assumes timely inclusion and bounded reorgs. Protocol details ↗

FROST DKG and nonces use the Zcash Foundation libraries; the adaptor extension and the Solidity check are this project’s own.

Why Zatomic

What other routes give up for a private trade

Most cross-chain systems optimize for liquidity, routing or general interoperability. Zatomic is built for something else: direct settlement of an agreed trade between two counterparties, with native ZEC kept shielded. It needs no solver or shared liquidity pool, exposes no shielded payout through a public viewing key, and asks Ethereum to verify no Zcash history: one adaptor secret connects two native settlements.

  • SPV, light-client or ZK bridge

    Strength: Ethereum verifies Zcash payments and events, which supports general cross-chain apps.

    For a private tradeEthereum must verify Zcash history. Skyhook’s September testnet swap put the Zcash recipient and txid on Ethereum (post ↗); hiding them with ZK needs extra proving infrastructure.

  • NEAR Intents

    Strength: competing solvers, many assets and automatic liquidity; live today.

    For a private tradeSettlement runs through solvers and bridge contracts. The cited Zcash payout code uses a public outgoing viewing key, exposing a shielded payout’s recipient and amount (code ↗).

  • THORChain

    Strength: native ZEC liquidity and market-priced swaps, with no wrapped ZEC.

    For a private tradeIts ZEC integration uses transparent Zcash transactions, so addresses and amounts are public, and funds pass through shared vaults (THORChain ↗).

Zatomic · direct shielded settlement

One scalar. Two native chains. No cross‑chain state proof.

One adaptor secret connects a shielded RedPallas claim to an Ethereum escrow: the secret t that releases the ETH completes Bob’s Zcash signature. Neither chain verifies the other’s transactions.

  • No third-party custodian or solverA 2-of-2 FROST key controls the ZEC; the escrow pays the ETH only to Alice with t, or back to Bob after the deadline.
  • Shielded when it settlesBoth Zcash transactions stay shielded on a settled swap, and no Zcash transaction ID, address or note reaches Ethereum.
  • Exits before fundingBob holds an adaptor pre-signature, Alice a fully signed, time-locked refund; each app resumes from its own records after a crash.

Proven on public testnets: between the Zcash testnet (Ironwood) and Sepolia, a swap settled and a no-reveal trade refunded both sides, first on 2026-10-18 and 10-20 and again on 2026-10-21 with the shipped V2 escrow (transactions). The live demo runs settlement, crash recovery and refunds in your browser.

Trade-offs: built for trades two parties have already agreed, so no price discovery or matching; ZEC ⇄ ETH only; each side watches until its deadline.
